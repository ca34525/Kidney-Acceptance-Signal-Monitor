# Waiting-list viability: continue the descriptive direction

**Plan 0025 verdict: continue.** A growing waiting list often coexists with increased recorded
transplant removals at the same program. This pattern meets the rules fixed before comparison
and recurs across 2023–2025. It supports examining registrations and removal categories together
when interpreting list growth. It does not authorize a predictive model or product change.

Among eligible programs with growing lists, transplant removals increased at **56 of 106 programs
in 2023 (52.8%)**, **54 of 114 in 2024 (47.4%)**, and **58 of 137 in 2025 (42.3%)**. For those
programs, median annual list growth was **26.5, 22.5 and 35 registrations**, respectively.
Median growth per 100 registrations at the current year's start was **9.3, 9.9 and 11.5**.
Every year exceeds the fixed 25% prevalence, 10-registration and 5-per-100 magnitude rules.

Both growing-list groups added more registrations at their medians. In the group with more
transplant removals, median additions increased by 20, 16.5 and 23 events; transplant removals
increased by 15, 12 and 8. In the same/fewer-removals group, median additions increased by
7.5, 7.5 and 11, while transplant removals changed by −5, −11 and −10. These separately calculated
medians cannot be summed into an accounting identity. Each program's saved calculation does
reconcile exactly. Changes in these counts explain **acceleration or slowing of growth**;
current growth itself is ending minus starting registrations.

The finding survives both planned checks. Among the same 169 eligible programs, increased
transplant removals accompanied 50.0%, 46.5% and 40.6% of growing lists, with all magnitude rules
passing. Across starting-list sizes below 100, 100–499 and at least 500, the program-weighted
shares were 43.7%, 38.9% and 59.8%; normalized growth also passed in each group.

**Practical consequence:** a program analyst should review registration demand and list-management
records alongside transplant activity before interpreting a larger list as fewer transplant
removals. Where removals fell, examine the transplant activity and its circumstances. Keep death,
deterioration, transplant elsewhere, transfer, recovery and other removals visible. A shrinking
list alone does not establish improvement. This screen identifies arithmetic patterns, not their
clinical causes, care quality or the appropriateness of an organ decision.

The source gate supports 2017–2025 from seven verified report releases. All 2,144 selected
program-years reconcile, but 241 consecutive pairs have discontinuous boundary counts and 11
have zero current starting counts; these were excluded. The selected three years retain
83.9%, 84.7% and 88.4% of matched programs. Two releases lack verified historical period
evidence and are excluded. Counts represent registration/removal events, not nationally unique
people or independently verified procedures. Repeated years are not independent validation.
