Kidney patient-journey prediction

What earlier public reports tell us about later recorded outcomes

V2 study and exploratory follow-up

Original V2 and completed exploratory follow-up.

Public aggregate research prototype. Not clinical or regulatory decision support.

Speaking notes and sources

A kidney program's quality-improvement reviewer needs a clear account of what a public signal measures. V1 reviews offer acceptance. V2 asks whether earlier public reports help predict the later percentage of listed candidates known alive with a functioning transplant. The main finding is that a large apparent model improvement depended on a weak comparison model. A separate investigation clarifies the unknown part of the published outcome. Neither study establishes clinical quality or effects of care. This story concerns programs and listing groups, not individual predictions.

Speaking notes and sources

One row describes a kidney transplant program and a July–June listing group. The target is the reported percentage known alive with a functioning transplant 18 months after listing. The denominator is the original listing group, including candidates who never received a transplant. This differs from survival among transplant recipients. Unknown status remains unknown and the published total stays authoritative. The model uses a fixed empirical-logit transform during fitting, then returns to published percentage points for evaluation. A hypothetical prediction of 40 percent against a reported 30 percent has an error of ten percentage points. The outcome is not officially risk adjusted and cannot support a fair-center ranking.

Public aggregate research prototype. Not clinical or regulatory decision support.

Speaking notes and sources

A measure has an observation period and a publication date. Both must satisfy the contract. Knowing that a report is old does not prove the outcome inside it was available at an earlier prediction date. V2 requires every predictor measurement end to precede the target listing group's start, and every predictor and training outcome to have been public by the prediction origin. The original ledger retains exact source dates and their precision. The July 2022 release has no verified publication day. The target starts in that month, so this is not a claim that the model predicted before any candidate entered the listing cohort.

Speaking notes and sources

A time-ordered split is often called a temporal fold. Although the panel contains four non-overlapping listing groups, earlier origins lack a configured earlier training outcome that was already public. Only the last origin can train Ridge. The 2026 source switches the target to calendar 2023, overlapping the previous July–June group, so it is excluded from the primary panel. Programs missing the later target remain in the audit as unknown outcomes. One inspected period cannot show stable performance across time.

Mean absolute error is the average size of an error without its sign. The history-only Ridge model has average error 11.488 percentage points. Adding acceptance gives 7.348. Speaking notes and sources

P3 matches the same program and listing group before describing associations. It reports every original approach in fixed order. Functioning and unknown percentages share a denominator and use mutually exclusive statuses. Signed error also subtracts the observed outcome, so correlation cannot identify reporting as a cause. Speaking notes and sources

The editorial selection rule sorts the original eligible programs by composite key and chooses the first with OAR above one, lower later functioning percentage and complete components.
