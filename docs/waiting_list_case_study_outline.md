# Five-minute explanation: follow the waiting-list counts

**0:00–0:40 — Question.** “A bigger kidney waiting list does not tell us whether transplant
activity fell. I wanted to show how additions and different removal categories change together,
using public annual records a program analyst can inspect.”

**0:40–1:20 — Data and safeguards.** One record is a program and calendar year, identified by
center code and type. Counts are registration/removal events, not unique people nationally.
Use the earliest verified report version. Require both annual equations to balance, continuous
year boundaries and a positive current starting list. The three comparisons are 2023–2025;
their current-year counts were published in July of the following year.

**1:20–2:10 — Finding and qualification.** Show the frequency figure: 56/106, 54/114 and 58/137
growing-list programs had more transplant removals — 52.8%, 47.4% and 42.3%. Then show the signed
distributions. Across all growing programs, median changes were +2, 0 and −2 events. Among only
those with increases, the medians were +15, +12 and +8. Some increases were just one event.
Frequency and size answer different questions. Per-100 values use current starting registrations;
they are not probabilities or percent changes from the prior transplant count.

**2:10–3:30 — Worked example.** Open ARUA:TX1's brief. In 2024: 155 + 288 − 180 = 263, so
growth was 108. In 2025: 263 + 288 − 232 = 319, so growth was 56. Transplant removals increased
from 144 to 168. The list grew while transplant removals increased, but growth slowed by 52.
Show the separate contributions: additions stayed unchanged; all eight removal changes together
account for that slowing. Each normalized contribution uses the same 263 starting registrations.
The fixed middle-record rule chose this example, not a search for a dramatic story.

**3:30–4:15 — Practical interpretation.** “The brief tells an analyst which recorded counts to
follow up.” Review additions against listing records, transplant removals against activity and
its circumstances, and other removals against their recorded reasons. Mention PAAG:TX1:
its list shrank while transplant removals fell and deaths/deterioration removals rose. A shrinking
list alone does not establish improvement. These are review questions, not demonstrated causes.

**4:15–5:00 — Limits and project history.** Exclusions reduce coverage to about 84–88% of matched
programs in these years. Common-program and size-group findings are retained from the completed
screen. Previously inspected aggregate outcomes are not fresh validation. Earlier forecasting
work is project history: V1 retained persistence, original V2 promoted no model, and the later
acceptance extension missed its fixed continuation rule. This case study deliberately completes
a descriptive question; it claims neither publication novelty nor proven decision benefit.
End on the reusable offline brief and the nonclinical/nonregulatory statement.
